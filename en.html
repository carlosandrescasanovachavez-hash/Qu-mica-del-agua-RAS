Water quality
in salmon RAS

Learn, step by step, which water parameters decide whether a salmon grows healthy or gets sick, and how to control them in a recirculating system.

Step 1 · The system

What is a RAS?

A Recirculating Aquaculture System reuses the same water again and again. It is cleaned with mechanical filters, a bacterial biofilter, degassing and oxygenation, and goes back to the tanks.

  • 90%More than 90% of the water is recirculated every day.
  • ⟳Everything that goes in (feed) stays in the water unless it is removed.
Step 2 · The problem

Everything is connected

Feed generates ammonia, CO₂ and solids. The biofilter consumes oxygen and alkalinity. If you change the pH, you change ammonia toxicity. That is why parameters must be looked at together.

  • NH₃Ammonia: toxic even in very small amounts.
  • O₂Oxygen: salmon need plenty of it.
  • pHpH controls the balance between CO₂ and ammonia.
  • TGPTotal gases and nitrogen (N₂): in excess they form bubbles inside the fish.

05 · Biofilter

MBBR biofilter model and nitrification

In a moving bed biofilm reactor (MBBR), thousands of plastic pieces (biocarriers) spin, driven by air. A biofilm of bacteria grows on them and turns toxic TAN into nitrate. Follow the molecules: they enter as ammonium (NH₄⁺), become nitrite (NO₂⁻) and leave as nitrate (NO₃⁻). Drag to rotate the model and turn off aeration to see what happens.

Step 1

Nitritation · AOB bacteria

NH₄⁺ + 1.5 O₂ → NO₂⁻ + 2H⁺ + H₂O

Nitrosomonas and Nitrosospira (also ammonia-oxidizing archaea). Consumes 3.43 g O₂ per g N and releases the acidity that uses up alkalinity.

Step 2

Nitratation · NOB bacteria

NO₂⁻ + 0.5 O₂ → NO₃⁻

Nitrospira and Nitrobacter. Consumes 1.14 g O₂ per g N. They are more sensitive to sudden changes: when they fail, a nitrite peak appears.

Overall

Full balance

NH₄⁺ + 2 O₂ → NO₃⁻ + 2H⁺ + H₂O
2H⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O

Excluding biomass: 4.57 g O₂ and 7.14 g CaCO₃ per g N. With cell synthesis (Ebeling et al. 2006): 4.18 g O₂, 7.05 g CaCO₃, 5.85 g CO₂ and ~0.20 g biomass. Some Nitrospira (comammox) perform both steps.

What nitrification needs

O₂> 4 mg/L in the reactor

O₂ has to cross the biofilm. With high TAN or a thick biofilm, oxygen becomes limiting before ammonia does.

pH7.0 – 8.0

Below 6.5 the rate drops sharply. Lowering pH to reduce NH₃ costs biofilter capacity.

Alkalinity> 70 mg/L CaCO₃

Summerfelt et al. (2015) measured better TAN removal at 70–200 than at 10 mg/L in MBBRs.

Temperaturestable

In cold water rates are lower and start-up slower. Sudden changes matter more than the absolute value.

Salinitygradual changes

Abrupt salinity increases inhibit nitrification, especially nitrite oxidation (Kinyage et al. 2019). Raise it in stages.

Organic matterremove solids

With more organic carbon, heterotrophs grow faster and displace nitrifiers. Remove solids before the biofilter.

Treatmentsdose carefully

Formalin, hydrogen peroxide and antibiotics can temporarily inhibit nitrification. Monitor TAN and nitrite afterwards.

Timeweeks

Nitrifiers grow slowly. A new biofilter in cold water can take 6 to 10 weeks or more to mature.

    How is it calculated?

    Dose (mJ/cm²) = Average intensity (mW/cm²) × Time (s)

    Time is the chamber volume (≈ 71 L) divided by the flow. Average intensity drops when the water absorbs light (low UVT) or the quartz is dirty. Reduction is estimated as log = Dose / D₁₀, where D₁₀ is the dose that kills 90% of that microorganism.

    Simplified model for teaching. Real units are validated with biodosimetry (for example USEPA UVDGM / ÖNORM protocols) and use safety factors.

    Where it goes in a RAS

    After the mechanical filter and the biofilter, before the water returns to the tanks. That way the water arrives clean (high UVT) and light is not wasted on particles. It is also used on the farm’s intake water and on egg water.

    Medium pressure vs low pressure

    Lamps with medium pressure emit a broad spectrum (200–400 nm) at high intensity: few lamps treat a lot of flow and make it harder for microorganisms to repair the damage. Lamps with low pressure emit almost only 254 nm and use less energy per lamp but many more are needed.

    What lowers the dose

    Turbid or coloured water (low UVT), dirty quartz, old lamps (they lose 20–30% of output at end of life), flow above design and cold water in low-pressure lamps.

    UV and ozone together

    When ozone is used, the UV reactor goes after it: UV light breaks down the residual ozone that would remain in the water and is toxic to salmon even at hundredths of a mg/L.

    08 · Background chemistry

    Reactions that explain the interactions

    Carbonate system

    CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ ⇌ 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻

    pK₁ ≈ 6.45 and pK₂ ≈ 10.45 at 12 °C. With pH between 6 and 8.3 almost all alkalinity is HCO₃⁻, so CO₂ = HCO₃⁻·[H⁺]/K₁: for each unit pH drops, free CO₂ is multiplied by ~10 at the same alkalinity.

    Ammonium / ammonia equilibrium

    NH₄⁺ ⇌ NH₃ + H⁺    f = 1 / (10^(pKa − pH) + 1)

    pKa = 0.0902 + 2729.92/(T + 273.2) ≈ 9.67 at 12 °C. Every 0.3 pH units roughly doubles the NH₃ fraction. Salinity slightly raises pKa and reduces NH₃.

    Nitrification with biomass synthesis

    NH₄⁺ + 1.83 O₂ + 1.97 HCO₃⁻ → 0.024 C₅H₇O₂N + 0.976 NO₃⁻ + 2.90 H₂O + 1.86 CO₂

    Per gram of TAN-N: 4.18 g O₂, 7.05 g alkalinity (CaCO₃) and 5.85 g CO₂ (Ebeling et al. 2006). Without biomass, the classic theoretical values are 4.57 g O₂ and 7.14 g CaCO₃.

    Ferrous iron oxidation

    4Fe²⁺ + O₂ + 10H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃↓ + 8H⁺

    Consumes ~0.14 mg O₂ and ~1.8 mg alkalinity (CaCO₃) per mg Fe²⁺. Fe(OH)₃ forms rust-coloured flocs that settle on gills and on the egg chorion.

    Ozone: useful oxidation and by-products

    O₃ + NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ + O₂  ·  O₃ + Br⁻ → OBr⁻ + O₂ → … BrO₃⁻

    In freshwater it oxidizes nitrite, dissolved organic matter and colour. With bromide (brackish water made with sea salt) it forms residual bromine oxidants and bromate, which persist longer than ozone itself.

    Aluminium speciation

    Al³⁺ ⇌ Al(OH)²⁺ ⇌ Al(OH)₂⁺ ⇌ Al(OH)₃↓ ⇌ Al(OH)₄⁻

    Minimum solubility near pH 6–6.5. The danger comes when acidic water containing Al mixes or reaches the gill (a more alkaline microenvironment): Al polymerizes and precipitates on the epithelium.

    09 · Sensitivity by life stage

    Not all life stages tolerate the same

    Green / eyed egg

    No gills: it exchanges gases through the chorion. Solids, Fe(OH)₃ and precipitates suffocate it. It needs very clean water (< 2 NTU), high O₂ and no residual ozone.

    Alevin / yolk sac

    Newly formed gills, very sensitive to NH₃, metals and supersaturated gas. Calcium in the water matters for osmoregulation.

    Fry / parr

    Higher tolerance, but chronic CO₂ > 15–20 mg/L reduces growth and promotes nephrocalcinosis.

    Smolt

    Smoltification depends on gill Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Labile Al and Cu at µg/L levels can ruin seawater tolerance even without visible mortality.

    Limitations

    The ranges for BOD, turbidity and ORP, and the biofilter surface removal rate, are operational references from the RAS industry, with less experimental support than those for NH₃, CO₂, O₂ or Al. Cu and Al toxicity depends on hardness, pH and dissolved organic carbon, so a single number does not describe it well. For discharges, the Chilean legal framework is D.S. 90/2000 and each farm’s RCA (environmental permit); check the applicable limits there. Every health decision must be validated with the farm’s own data and with the veterinarian.
